A microscope setup a researcher can switch in seconds

A cancer researcher had to take her imaging setup apart every time she changed configurations. Recurring interviews, cheap prototypes, and check-ins that caught what I'd missed led to a full redesign: one slide, about 5 seconds, nothing to take apart.

01

The user and her problem

My client images mice with a portable LED microscope that runs in two configurations. To switch between them, she had to remove four delicate LED fibers and two stages, then put everything back together. Each change took 3 to 4 minutes of careful hands-on work, every time, and every time the fibers were at risk.

She didn't need a more powerful microscope. She needed the switch to stop being a chore, while everything else about her setup stayed the way she already worked.

Let her switch configurations in seconds, with no tools and nothing to take apart, without getting in the way of the mouse.

02

Process

The first concept failed in a check-in, and that was the most useful meeting of the summer.

  1. Listen first May 2026

    Recurring interviews with my client about how she actually uses the microscope, what each configuration is for, and what switching between them involves.

  2. Turn it into prioritized needs

    I translated her workflow into a ranked list of needs, each with a measurable target (minimum, nominal, and ideal), so we could agree on what "good enough" meant before I designed anything.

  3. Prototype cheap, show early

    I brought low-fidelity prototypes to our check-ins instead of finished parts, so she could react to something physical while changes were still cheap.

  4. Catch what I missed

    Reacting to the prototypes in our check-ins, she surfaced two requirements I had missed: she needed clear access to the mouse during imaging, and the viewing angle had to stay the same. My first concept couldn't meet either one.

  5. Redesign from scratch

    Instead of patching the first concept, I started over around those two needs. The new design keeps the center open for the mouse and keeps the viewing angle she needs.

  6. Model, print, and test Jul 2026

    Modeled the full assembly in Onshape, 3D printed it, and tested it against her needs: switch time, adjustment in 3D, room for the mouse, and fit with her existing setup.

03

The final design

The whole interaction is three moves: unlock, slide, lock. A slider carries the cup of LEDs between the two configurations, one at each end of its travel. At the top you lock it by sliding left, and at the bottom by sliding right. There are no tools, screws, or electronics, and the fibers never come out.

04

Results

  • A configuration change went from 3 to 4 minutes to under 5 seconds.
  • Both missed requirements are met: clear access to the mouse and a fixed viewing angle.
  • It fits her existing setup, so nothing else about her workflow had to change.

What I'd take to any product: show something rough early. The requirement that reshapes a design usually comes out when the user is holding a prototype, not when they're answering questions.
